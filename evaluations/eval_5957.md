## Verdict
**SKIP | 3/10** — Open-source curiosity with no clear payer, high legal risk, and no moat for a solo developer chasing fast cash.

## Pain & evidence
- Pain: Large language models often refuse safe prompts, write long bland answers (“slop”), and agree with users even when wrong (“sycophancy”). Fixing this normally requires expensive retraining.
- Evidence: 59,963 GitHub stars show developer curiosity, not buyer intent. Most people interested in uncensored models run them locally for free. No public data shows willingness to pay monthly for this. Indian developers especially expect free tools.

## Target buyer
- Who pays: Indie AI app builders and solo founders who want their chatbot to sound less corporate and refuse less often. A few small customer-support teams might care about tone.
- Where they hang out: X/Twitter, Discord AI servers, Reddit communities such as r/LocalLLaMA and r/MachineLearning, IndieHackers, GitHub discussions.
- Reality check: Buyers are price-sensitive and technical. Enterprises will avoid “remove censorship” because of legal and brand risk.

## Competition & wedge
| Alternative | Cost | How this differs |
| Open-source prompt lists | Free | Packaged as one toggle, no setup |
| Fine-tuning services | ₹8,000–₹40,000 per job | No training needed, works on any model |
| Research steering code | Free | Claims to be plug-and-play, not a notebook |

## Monetization
- Pricing: ₹299/month ($4) basic; ₹999/month ($12) pro with remote access for other apps.
- First-month revenue: ₹0 to ₹15,000 ($0 to $180). Expect closer to zero.
- Payment: UPI and Stripe India.

## Distribution
Exact 3 channels:
1. ProductHunt launch with an X thread tagging AI founders.
2. Reddit posts in r/LocalLLaMA and r/SelfHosted (no paid ads).
3. Direct messages to 20 IndieHackers who ship AI tools.

## Risks & kill-criteria
- Risks: Liability if the model outputs harmful or illegal content after censorship is removed; app stores may ban it; bigger AI labs release a free toggle that does the same; your steering breaks when the model updates.
- Kill-criteria: Under 10 sign-ups or zero paying users in 14 days. Any legal notice or app-store rejection. Monthly server cost higher than revenue.

## 7-day MVP plan
- Day 1: Download one small open model (around 8 billion parameters) and run it on a laptop or cheap cloud graphics processor.
- Day 2: Build a basic chat page. Add two switches: “Less bland answers” and “Fewer refusals.” Use only system instructions and prompt tricks, no retraining.
- Day 3: Add a third switch for “Less agreement” (reduce sycophancy). Test on ten real-world prompts.
- Day 4: Wrap it into a simple website with a login and a payment button.
- Day 5: Fix crashes. Make sure responses load in under ten seconds.
- Day 6: Publish a landing page with pricing. Connect UPI and Stripe.
- Day 7: Launch on ProductHunt, Reddit, and X. Send direct messages to indie builders.

## Final call
Skip this idea. The audience wants free open-source software, the legal exposure is serious, and a solo developer in India cannot build a defensible micro-product fast enough to earn real cash.
