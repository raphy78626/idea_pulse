## Verdict
WATCH · 4/10 · Beautiful output, but zero buyer signal and heavy competition make it a hobby, not a fast-cash business.

## Pain & evidence
Founders record phone or computer screens for landing pages. The videos look amateur: shaky cursor, plain background, no polish. Apple-style ads use slow zooms, soft shadows, device frames and clean text. Making that takes video-editing skill and hours.
Evidence so far: the Show HN post has 6 points and zero comments. That is not buyer interest. No waiting list, no pre-sales, no paying user mentioned.

## Target buyer
Who pays: solo software founders, tool makers and indie marketers who need a feature-launch video for a website or social post.
Where they hang out: X (Twitter), Indie Hackers, Product Hunt, small founder Discord groups and LinkedIn launch posts.
They pay from personal savings or a small company card, in rupees or dollars.

## Competition & wedge

| Alternative | Price | Weakness | My wedge |
|-------------|-------|----------|----------|
| Screen Studio | $99 one-time | Mac only, steep learning curve | Works in browser, no install |
| Loom | Free to $12/month | Built for calls, not marketing | Cinematic frames and motion |
| Canva | Free to $15/month | Generic templates, slow uploads | Purpose-built for screen recordings |

## Monetization
One-time fee fits fast cash better than a subscription.
- Single export: ₹499 (~$6)
- Pack of five exports: ₹1,499 (~$18)
- First video free with a small watermark

Expected first-month revenue: ₹0 to ₹30,000 ($0 to $360). Most unknown solo launches make under ₹10,000 without an existing audience.

## Distribution (exact 3 channels)
1. X (Twitter): post 10-second before/after reels of real products, tag founders, offer to re-style one of their screen recordings free.
2. Product Hunt and Indie Hackers: launch as a "launch-day video maker" with a clear free tier.
3. Direct messages: find 50 Gumroad or Lemon Squeezy sellers with weak demo videos, send a fixed sample of their own recording re-styled.

## Risks & kill criteria
- Rendering video costs server money; each export can cost more than the fee if not controlled.
- "Apple-style" is a look, not a patent; big video tools can copy it in weeks.
Kill criteria: no paying customer after 30 days; fewer than 10 signups after two launches; cost to render one video exceeds ₹100.

## 7-day MVP plan (minimum viable product, day-by-day)
Day 1 – Record one raw screen demo and edit it by hand into the Apple look. Note every click, zoom and text step.
Day 2 – Build a one-page website where a user uploads an MP4 and picks a phone or laptop frame.
Day 3 – Add automatic cursor smoothing and a soft background gradient using simple video tools.
Day 4 – Add a download button and a payment link (Stripe or UPI) for ₹499 per export.
Day 5 – Ask five founder friends to test it. Fix what confuses them.
Day 6 – Make two before/after videos using real products from Day 5 testers.
Day 7 – Post on X and Product Hunt, and send 10 direct messages with free samples.

## Final call
Do not quit your day job. Treat this as a two-week experiment; if no one pays by day 14, park it and sell a done-for-you demo service instead.
