## Verdict
**SKIP | 3/10** — Custom onboarding art is freelancing, not a product; seven of nine buyers quit on the same question, so demand is unproven and weak.

## Pain & evidence
Small software teams need friendly welcome screens, empty states, and step graphics when a new user logs in. Most founders are not designers. They either use generic free clip art or leave screens blank, which makes new users quit.

Evidence: nine sales calls. Two paid ₹600 each. Seven stopped at the same objection—likely price or “what exactly do I get?” That pattern means the offer is unclear and the price is too low to build a business.

## Target buyer
**Who pays:** Founders and product people at early-stage software teams (5–20 people) who just launched or are about to launch.

**Where they hang out:** LinkedIn, Twitter/X, IndieHackers, Product Hunt, niche Slack and Discord groups for startup founders.

## Competition & wedge
| Alternative | What they do | Weakness | Your wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fiverr illustrators | Cheap custom drawings | Slow, uneven quality | Fast, onboarding-specific packs with copy |
| Free illustration sites | Generic drawings | Not tied to your product | Art matched to your actual screens |
| Design agencies | Full branding | Expensive, slow | Small fixed-price deliverable in 48 hours |

## Monetization
Price per pack of five onboarding illustrations: **₹3,000** (about $35). Custom single screen: **₹8,000** (about $95). Do not charge ₹600; that is hobby money.

Expected first-month revenue: **₹0 to ₹20,000** ($0 to $250), most likely under ₹5,000.

## Distribution
1. Cold direct messages on LinkedIn and Twitter to founders who launched in the last 30 days, offering a free onboarding screenshot review.
2. Launch a free sample pack on Product Hunt and IndieHackers with a Gumroad payment link.
3. Bundle your art as a paid add-on with no-code software boilerplate sellers.

## Risks & kill-criteria
- Buyers see it as one-off design work, not a repeat purchase.
- Global buyers can get similar art cheaper on Fiverr; Indian buyers will balk above ₹1,000.
- No moat: another illustrator can copy your style in a week.

**Kill if:** You do not get three paying customers at ₹3,000 or more within 30 days, or if it takes more than five days to deliver one order.

## 7-day MVP plan
(MVP = simple first test version)

Day 1: Choose one niche, for example HR software. Draw three sample screens. Set up a simple payment page.

Day 2: Write a one-line offer: “Five onboarding illustrations plus welcome copy, delivered in 48 hours for ₹3,000.”

Day 3: List 50 founder profiles. Send 20 short direct messages with a free sample attached.

Day 4: Post the sample pack on Product Hunt and IndieHackers.

Day 5: Follow up with everyone who did not reply. Add a money-back guarantee.

Day 6: Close any deals. Deliver the first order within 48 hours.

Day 7: Count revenue. If under ₹5,000, stop or switch to selling reusable packs.

## Final call
Skip this as a custom art service; it is freelancing without leverage, and seven lost calls show the value is unclear. Only come back if you sell reusable illustration packs at scale, not one-off commissions.
